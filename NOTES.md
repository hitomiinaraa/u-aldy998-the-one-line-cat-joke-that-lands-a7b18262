The joke opens with a small, very human plea: a person asking a pet for a little breathing room.
A cat, of course, treats a spoken boundary as an invitation to test it at once.
The payoff is the animal doing the precise opposite of what was asked, with total commitment.
There is no setup beyond the request itself, so the laugh arrives in a single beat.
The humor comes from how familiar the behavior is to anyone who has lived with a cat.
Cats do not negotiate, they occupy, and the line lets that trait do all the work.
The wording stays plain and conversational, which keeps the scene instantly recognizable.
Nothing in the line explains itself; the reader supplies the image of a cat settling in.
That unspoken picture is what makes the ending land harder than any extra detail would.
The whole approach fits the short limit because the joke needs only one request and one refusal.
Brevity suits a cat joke because the punchline lands before the reader can brace for it.
